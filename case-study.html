The operational problem

A repair rarely follows a straight line. Inspection changes the estimate, a customer may decline, a part may be unavailable, and mechanics have limited capacity. A progress indicator alone does not manage those decisions. Northline connects them to saved records and explicit business rules.

A working application

The workshop contains twelve priced repair types, searchable and filtered repairs, mechanic assignment, a capacity-aware schedule, stock receipts and reservations, quality checks, offline payment records and operational reports. The illustrated website shares the same saved workspace. A private Node installation adds operator sign-in and custom customer/bike intake.

Prices that retain context

New repairs snapshot a starting service price. Inspection confirms the actual charge, work description and compatible parts; the server calculates quantities and totals. Every revision retains earlier totals and decisions, and the customer must approve the exact current version before work continues. Catalog edits reach the website without changing historical quotes.

Parts and capacity

Approval reserves available parts; competing jobs cannot claim the same units. Shortages pause work until stock arrives. Completion consumes inventory and records movements. Scheduling separates work dates, optional job-specific bench budgets and expected collection. Unknown budgets stay visible. Safety checks gate release, with a documented shifting exception for bikes without gear shifting.

Recoverable saves

Confirmation follows a saved update. Intake references prevent duplicates, and conditional revisions reject stale changes from another tab. Lost responses block further writes until a refresh reveals the stored outcome. Saved private records survive sign-out and restart.

Practical verification

Domain/server tests cover business rules and failure boundaries. Browser journeys exercise the actual application in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit, including two narrow widths, exports, printing, private sign-in, keyboard focus and unsuppressed accessibility scans. The repository retains the acceptance plan, evidence and operating instructions.

Try the whole repair

  1. Open Services & prices and create a Brake service repair.
  2. Assign a mechanic and date, inspect it, and revise the estimate with fitting adjustment, brake pads and two inner tubes: $109 without collection.
  3. Approve in Customer view, then return to Workshop view to complete the work and four checks.
  4. Record an offline payment, mark collection, reload and inspect Reports and the stock ledger.
Clear product boundaries

Northline is fictional and no client or revenue results are claimed. The hosted portfolio uses sample data and openly accessible workflow views. The private app uses one operator key; Customer view records a communicated decision rather than verifying a separate identity. Payment records do not charge cards, summaries are not tax invoices, and email, purchasing and staff permissions are outside this release.
