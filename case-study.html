The problem

A rider needs to choose suitable care, understand the labour estimate, and know whether a repair is waiting for approval or ready for collection. A workshop needs a consistent sequence of steps. This fictional brief brings those two needs into one demonstration.

The solution

The service menu filters six options and calculates collection costs. Submitting a sample request creates a repair in that visitor’s workspace. The workshop view moves it through inspection, then pauses for customer approval. After approval, the repair continues through work, a ride check, and collection.

Clear decisions

Labour prices come from the server’s catalogue. The workflow blocks unavailable actions and records each completed stage. The next responsible person is labelled beside the action. Inline controls switch views while keeping the same repair open. Ready and collected repairs have separate counts and filters; estimates show labour and collection costs separately.

Reliable feedback

Confirmation follows a saved update. Repeated requests use the same request reference to avoid duplicate repairs. Conflicting updates prompt a reload rather than overwriting newer progress. An unconfirmed save blocks further actions until progress reloads. Service-menu failures have a retry control, and request choices remain locked during submission.

Appropriate demo data

The form accepts fixed sample choices. It collects no names, email addresses, or free-text notes. Each workspace holds up to ten repairs and expires after seven days, with automatic cleanup of expired samples.

Practical verification

The accompanying QA work documents reproducible defects and corrections. Regression checks exercise validation, approval, persistence, duplicate requests, visitor separation, conflicting updates, and expiry. GitHub repeats the complete browser journey at desktop and two phone widths, including lost-response retries, competing tabs, failure recovery, and automated accessibility checks. These checks use fresh local sample workspaces.

What this demonstrates

  • Turning a business brief into a working website and service workflow.
  • Original visual design with responsive layouts, labelled controls, and visible keyboard focus.
  • Connecting a request form to persistent state, with validation and clear recovery when a request fails.
  • Testing behavior and documenting the evidence and limitations.
Project scope

This is an independent portfolio demonstration. Northline is fictional, and no client relationship, revenue, or customer results are claimed. Customer and workshop views are available to every visitor; they demonstrate workflow rather than authenticated accounts. The project does not book appointments, send email, or take payment.

Explore it yourself

  1. Create a sample repair with a bike, concern, and service.
  2. Open its tracker and start an inspection in Workshop view.
  3. Request approval, use Continue in Customer view, and approve the estimate.
  4. Return to Workshop view and complete the repair. Reload to see its saved journal.
