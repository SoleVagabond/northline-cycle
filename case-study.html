The operational problem

A repair rarely follows a straight line. Inspection changes the estimate, a customer may decline, a part may be unavailable, and mechanics have limited capacity. A progress indicator alone does not manage those decisions. Northline connects them to saved records and explicit business rules.

A working application

The workshop connects customer and bike histories, configurable services and compatible part models, searchable repairs, staff availability, stock receipts and reservations, quality checks, offline payment records and reports. The illustrated website shares the same saved workspace. A private Node installation adds operator sign-in and custom customer/bike intake.

Prices that retain context

New repairs snapshot a starting service price. Inspection confirms itemized service lines, inspected charges and compatible parts; the server calculates quantities and exact cent totals. Every revision retains earlier totals and decisions, and the customer must approve the exact current version before work continues. Catalog edits reach the website without changing historical quotes.

Parts and capacity

Approval reserves available parts; competing jobs cannot claim the same units. Shortages pause work until stock arrives. Completion consumes inventory and records movements. Scheduling separates work dates, optional job-specific bench budgets and expected collection. Unknown budgets stay visible. Shop opening days, staff workdays and time off are configurable; existing conflicting plans are flagged for review. Safety checks gate release, with a documented shifting exception for bikes without gear shifting.

Records that stay connected

Customer and bike records connect returning visits without rewriting earlier repair snapshots. Condition, findings, customer updates and private/shared photos persist. Repair links grant a separate recipient access to one estimate and current decision. Confirmation follows a saved update. Intake references prevent duplicates, and conditional revisions reject stale changes from another tab. Lost responses block further writes until a refresh reveals the stored outcome. Saved private records survive sign-out and restart.

Practical verification

Domain/server tests cover business rules and failure boundaries. Browser journeys exercise the actual application in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit, including two narrow widths, exports, printing, private sign-in, keyboard focus and unsuppressed accessibility scans. The repository retains the acceptance plan, evidence and operating instructions.

Try the whole repair

  1. Open Services & prices and create a Brake service repair.
  2. Assign a mechanic and planned work date. Inspect, describe the work and enter a $60 service charge, compatible brake pads and two inner tubes: $109 without collection. Confirm the expected collection date separately.
  3. Create a customer repair link and open its separate page to approve the current estimate. Return to the workshop to complete work and the applicable safety checks.
  4. Record an offline payment, mark collection, reload and inspect Reports and the stock ledger.
Clear product boundaries

Northline is fictional and no client or revenue results are claimed. The hosted portfolio uses sample data and openly accessible workflow views. The private app uses one operator key. A separate expiring, revocable repair link authorizes a recipient by possession without verifying identity; internal notes and private photos stay outside that page. Payment records do not charge cards, summaries are not tax invoices, and email, purchasing and staff permissions are outside this release.
