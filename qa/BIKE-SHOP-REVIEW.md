# Bicycle-shop business review

Reviewed October 4, 2026. The goal is to correct the operating model, not count features. This is desk research using bike retailers, workshop software documentation and a bicycle-tool manufacturer. No shop owner was interviewed and no competitor software account was operated. Sources describe their own practices; they do not establish a universal shop policy or validate Northline's fictional rates.

## Corrections implemented

1. **Price presentation.** Catalog and illustrated service cards show starting prices and units. Quotes show a service charge and separately itemized parts. Repeating “labour” on each card has been removed. “Labour” remains an internal compatibility field for previously saved financial records; it is not a public label or a time calculation.
2. **Scope.** One-wheel puncture repair, minor wheel truing, one mechanical brake, one hydraulic brake, one cable and one accessible hub/headset are explicit. Full refresh does not include every rebuild, seized component, suspension job or replacement part. E-bike work is mechanical inspection; electrical diagnosis is excluded.
3. **Repair times.** Preset catalog durations and their automatic scheduling calculations have been removed. An optional bench budget is entered for the individual job. It is an internal planning estimate, not a rider-facing completion promise. Unknown budgets remain unknown and are counted separately in workload and calendar views.
4. **Dates.** Planned work and expected collection are separate fields. Collection can stay unconfirmed or be cleared when no realistic date is available. Scheduling notes with internal minutes are omitted from customer view and printed summaries. Collection estimates must be reviewed when stock, approval or findings change.
5. **Inspected quote.** Both quote editors accept the actual service charge and a description of the agreed work. Arbitrary “extra fitting” price presets are no longer offered. A changed work description requires fresh approval even when the total stays the same. Server pricing, part quantities, prior versions and stock reservations remain enforced.
6. **Parts.** New inspected quotes require a specification for each selected part. The sample inventory is explicitly described as categories, not universal SKUs. Specifications are recorded by the operator; this does not substitute for manufacturer guidance or a configurable, variant-specific inventory system.
7. **Safety checks.** A no-shifting bike can record a documented exception to the shifting check. Brakes, wheels/tyres and fasteners/ride check cannot be omitted using that exception. Recorded checks do not certify the physical repair or replace a trained mechanic's inspection.
8. **Existing records.** Historical service prices and approvals remain saved. Earlier jobs acquire no invented bench estimate or customer collection date. The existing date field is retained as planned work for storage compatibility; it is not relabeled as a promise to the customer.

These decisions are design inferences from the sources, rather than a claim that all shops follow exactly this sequence. Real shops may offer quick walk-in repairs, fixed packages, hourly billing or item pricing. The app supports a bounded inspection-and-approval workflow, not every possible shop operation.

## Limits that still matter

Northline's prices are fictional and were not established by a local shop's costs, wages, margin, rent or market. The October 5 release makes services, actual part models/SKUs, staff availability and daily bench budgets configurable. Earlier sample categories remain explicitly labelled. A ceiling is not the mechanic's productive availability; breaks, intake, interruptions, skills and supplier lead times are not calculated. Part prices are sample values and configurable part records are not supplier procurement or an automated compatibility engine.

The single operator records workshop actions and can record communicated decisions in private mode. The October 5 release also supplies expiring, revocable repair links for a recipient to review and decide on their current estimate; possession grants access without verifying identity. Separate staff permissions and customer identities, outbound messages, appointment capacity, deposits, tax, refunds, purchase orders, part returns and accounting integrations are absent. Public mode uses fictional records. A usable portfolio workflow and passing tests do not establish readiness to run a commercial shop without those requirements being assessed with its operator.
